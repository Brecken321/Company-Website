Afterwards

Keeping it up to date.

The website is yours from the day we hand it over, whichever of these you choose. The only question is how you'd rather pay for changes later on.

Option one

No monthly cost

$0/month

  • Most small sites don't change much from one year to the next
  • If your hours and prices stay put, there's nothing to pay us
  • The website keeps working on its own
  • Need something changed later? Ask us and we'll price up the one-off
Option two

We make them for you

$10/month

  • The website stays in your name and on your account
  • You give us editing access — like a spare key you can take back
  • We can change anything on the website within 3 business days
  • Option to add a new page to the website every quarter
  • Cancel whenever, and nothing about your site changes

Either way, the website is yours and stays yours. We don't host it, we don't hold it, and we don't run it for you. The account is in your name from day one — the monthly option simply means changes are included rather than charged one at a time.

The small print, in plain English

Two costs that aren't ours.

Every website needs a domain name (the yourbusiness.com bit) and somewhere to live. Those are paid straight to the provider, not to us, and we don't take a cut or mark them up.

We'll set both up with you and put them in your name, so if you ever part ways with us you keep control of everything.

Money questions

The ones people actually ask.

When do I actually pay?

After you've seen the finished site and said you want it. We build first and show you — if it isn't right, you walk away owing nothing. Once you're happy, we invoice, and the files are yours.

Is there anything else to pay later?

Only the domain and hosting above, and those go to the provider rather than to us. There's no licence, no per-page charge and no platform subscription. If you take the $10 monthly option, that's the only thing you ever pay us.

What if I need more than ten pages?

That's fine — we'll quote for it before we start rather than surprising you at the end. Bigger jobs are priced on what's actually involved, and you'll have the number in writing first.

Can I pay in instalments?

Decide your answer here — splitting the fee in two, one payment at handover and one a month later, is a common arrangement and worth offering if you're comfortable with it.

Why is this cheaper than the quotes I've had?

Because we're a small family team without an agency's offices, salespeople and account managers to pay for — and because our build setup gets us to a working site quickly. You're paying for the work itself, not the overheads wrapped around it.

What if it isn't what I was hoping for?

Tell us, and we'll put it right — changes before launch are included. If it still isn't for you, you simply don't take it, and we part on good terms. That costs us some time, which is a risk we're happy to carry; it's a fairer deal than asking you to gamble a deposit on a website you haven't seen.
