After you say yes

Putting the site in your name.

Once you've told us you want the website, it becomes yours — properly yours, not rented from us. Here's exactly how that happens. It takes about ten minutes of your time, and you don't need to understand a word of code.

What you need

An email address you can get into. That really is all.

How long it takes

About ten minutes, spread over a day or two while emails go back and forth.

What it costs

Nothing. The account is free and so is the hosting.

Step by step

Five things, in order.

You do steps 1, 2 and 3. We do the rest. If any of it looks intimidating, ring us and we'll go through it together on the phone.

  1. 1

    Make a free GitHub account

    GitHub is the service that stores your website's files. Think of it as a filing cabinet that also happens to put the site online. It's free, and most weeks you won't need to touch it.

    Go to github.com/signup, put in your email address, choose a password, and pick a username. GitHub emails you a code to check it's really you.

    Pick the username carefully. Something like your business name is ideal — it can end up visible in your web address, and changing it later is a nuisance.

    About 3 minutes

  2. 2

    Turn on the extra security step

    GitHub will ask you to set up two-factor authentication. That just means it texts you a code, or you use an app on your phone, whenever you sign in somewhere new.

    It also gives you a list of recovery codes. Save them somewhere safe — print them, or put them in a drawer. They're how you get back in if you ever lose your phone.

    About 2 minutes

  3. 3

    Email us your username

    Send us the username you picked — just the username, nothing else. That's all we need to start the transfer.

    We will never ask for your password. Not by email, not on the phone, not ever. If anyone asks you for it — including someone claiming to be us — say no and call us.

    About 1 minute

  4. 4

    Accept the transfer email

    We start the handover from our side. GitHub then emails you something like “Repository transfer request”. Open it, click the button inside, and confirm.

    That's the moment the website legally changes hands. The link expires after about a day — if it runs out before you get to it, just tell us and we'll send another. Nothing is lost.

    About 2 minutes

  5. 5

    We put your web address on it

    Last part, and it's ours to do. We point your web address — yourbusiness.com, or whichever one you choose — at the site, check every page still works, and make sure the little padlock shows in the browser.

    The web address is registered in your name, on your own account, paid for by you (around $15 a year). We only ever set it up for you. That way nothing about your website depends on us still being around.

    Nothing for you to do

That's it — the website is yours. The files, the web address and the account are all in your name. You can hand it to any other developer tomorrow without asking us, and they'll be able to pick it straight up. Nothing is locked to us.

Common questions

Things people ask at this stage.

What actually is GitHub?

It's a website that stores files and puts websites online. Millions of businesses use it. For you it's mostly just a login you'll rarely need — but because the account is in your name, it's the thing that makes the website genuinely yours rather than borrowed from us.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Making an account and clicking a link in an email is the whole of your side. After that you don't need to touch the website at all — when something needs changing, just tell us.

Will GitHub start charging me?

No. What your site needs sits inside the free plan, and there's no card on the account to charge. GitHub sells paid plans for big software teams; that isn't you.

Can you still make changes for me afterwards?

Yes. The website stays in your name — you'd just add us as an editor on your account, the way you'd lend a spare key to a trusted tradesperson. You can remove us again whenever you like, and the site is unaffected either way. That's what the $10 a month option covers, though you're welcome to just call us when something comes up instead.

What if I forget my login?

Use the “forgot password” link and GitHub emails you a reset. That's why the recovery codes from step 2 are worth keeping somewhere safe — they're the backup if you also change your phone number.

What if I'd rather not have a GitHub account at all?

Then we'll put the site somewhere else instead — there are several good options and we'll walk you through them. It's your website; you shouldn't have to sign up to anything you're not comfortable with.

Can I move the website somewhere else later?

Any time, and you don't need our permission. It's ordinary web files, not a locked-in system. Download them and take them anywhere.

Stuck on any of it?

Call us and we'll do it together, one step at a time. It's a five minute phone call and we genuinely don't mind.
